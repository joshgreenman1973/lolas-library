This week's picks

September 5, 2026

A quieter week for stars but not for range: a myth saga barreling toward its climax, a blizzard that traps two sisters together, a street-art mystery, a detective baby graphic novel and a weightlifter drawing up her own training plan. Only one of the five picked up a confirmed starred review, and two are graphic novels — call it an honest snapshot of what actually published this week rather than a curated mix.

1

The Neverfear

Katherine Rundell
Fantasy / Adventure Ages 10–14 Starred · Kirkus Reviews

The third book in Katherine Rundell's blockbuster Impossible Creatures series returns to the hidden archipelago of Glimourie, where the mythical creatures living there are being hunted and an old evil is closing in. The young heroes who saved the archipelago once already have to do it again, faster and with higher stakes.

For Lola: If you loved the first two Impossible Creatures books, this is the one where everything you've been worried about finally comes due. Rundell writes like she's dared herself to make you gasp and tear up on the same page.
August 31, 2026 · Knopf · Kirkus: "Brilliant... a perfectly paced, mythology-laced tale of action, adventure, and heart"
Buy at Bookshop.org
2

Sisters Alone

Shifa Saltagi Safadi
Contemporary / Survival, novel in verse Ages 8–12

Two Syrian American sisters who can barely stand each other get trapped together when a sudden blizzard buries the world around them. Told in verse, it's a survival story that turns out to really be about what sisters owe each other when there's nowhere else to go.

For Lola: This one will wreck you a little, in the best way. It moves fast because it's written in verse, but it'll make you think about your own siblings differently by the last page.
September 8, 2026 · Putnam · 336 pp. · Publishers Weekly: "equal parts survival story and homage to sisterhood, it's a standout"
Buy at Bookshop.org
3

Art Attack

James Ponti
Mystery / Humor Ages 8–12

The Sherlock Society kids are back for a third case, chasing down a famous street artist who keeps striking during Miami Art Week without ever getting caught. It's the latest from the Edgar Award-winning author of the Framed! series.

For Lola: You don't need to have read the first two to jump in, but you'll want to after. Ponti writes puzzle-box mysteries that actually let you solve them if you're paying attention.
September 8, 2026 · Aladdin · Book three of the Sherlock Society, from the Edgar Award-winning author of Framed!
Buy at Bookshop.org
4

Detective Baby: Dance Team Drama

Meg Cabot
Humor / Graphic novel Ages 8–12

Jenny is stuck babysitting Rosie, a baby who happens to be the world's best (and most stubborn) detective, right when the money Jenny raised for her dance team goes missing. It's a new graphic novel series launch from Meg Cabot, illustrated by Amelia Allore.

For Lola: It's silly on purpose and that's the whole point. Perfect for a night when you want a mystery you can finish in one sitting and laugh through the whole thing.
September 1, 2026 · HarperAlley · Graphic novel · Kirkus: "a promising, character-driven series starter featuring the cutest supporting cast imaginable"
Buy at Bookshop.org
5

Raise the Bar

Fernanda Frick H.
Sports / Graphic novel Ages 8–12

Sam Sanchez is a self-taught weightlifter training with no coach and no real equipment until she gets a shot to train under her Olympic idol. This graphic novel debut is about how far you go for a dream nobody handed you.

For Lola: This is for anyone who's ever wanted something badly enough to make it up as they went along. The art alone is worth the read, it makes every lift feel like it matters.
August 2026 · Random House Graphic · Graphic novel · Publishers Weekly: "it all combines into a gold-medal performance"
Buy at Bookshop.org

August 29, 2026

1

Quetzal's Labyrinth

Karla Arenas Valenti
Fantasy / Adventure Ages 9–12 Starred · Kirkus and Publishers Weekly

Twelve-year-old Diego finds out the Aztec gods are real, and that one of them wants the world back. To stop it he has to go down into a labyrinth that rearranges itself around him, past monsters and riddles, toward a god-sized grudge waiting at the center.

For Lola: If you tore through Percy Jackson and wanted the same thing again but not the same, this is it — the mythology is Aztec, the stakes are real and the maze keeps moving under your feet. Kirkus called it one of the best middle-grade mythological fantasies going.
September 1, 2026 · Knopf · 352 pp. · Starred reviews in Kirkus and Publishers Weekly
Buy at Bookshop.org
2

Little Monsters

Máire Roche
Fantasy / Horror Ages 9–12 Starred · Publishers Weekly

The four March sisters from Little Women, except Meg's teeth are a little too sharp, Jo goes furry under a full moon, Beth flies at night and Amy grows a mermaid's tail near water. Meg cannot always remember what she does after dark, and when the neighbors start hunting for the monster in their midst she has to find the culprit before someone finds her sisters.

For Lola: A Little Women retelling where the sisters are literally monsters is such a good idea you'll be a little annoyed nobody thought of it sooner. Creepy enough to be fun, never so scary that you regret reading it at night.
August 18, 2026 · Aladdin · 288 pp. · Starred review in Publishers Weekly
Buy at Bookshop.org
3

Wish You Well

Jamie Sumner
Contemporary Ages 10 and up Starred · Publishers Weekly

Violet Marino is going blind and would very much like to be left alone about it, thank you, so she can play soccer. Then she falls in with two other kids carrying big diagnoses and bigger personalities, and the three of them decide to run a con on a wish-granting foundation.

For Lola: Three chronically ill kids conclude the wish charity owes them and set out to take it for everything they can get. It's funny in the specific way that only a book which flatly refuses to feel sorry for its characters can be.
September 1, 2026 · Atheneum · 288 pp. · Starred review in Publishers Weekly
Buy at Bookshop.org
4

Firebird Waltz

Misty Copeland
Contemporary / Fantasy Ages 9–12 Star Fire Academy, book one

Josie Davis and her best friend Patrick get into the Star Fire Academy for the Performing Arts in New York City, where the company is staging Stravinsky's Firebird and the competition for parts is brutal. Then Josie learns she is next in line to be the Firebird, and not in the casting sense.

For Lola: Audition nerves, a rival who is genuinely better than you and a myth that turns out to be load-bearing. Copeland was the first Black woman to be named principal dancer at American Ballet Theatre, and the studio scenes read like someone who has actually stood in one.
August 2026 · Aladdin · 240 pp. · Kirkus: "Myth comes to life in this fast-paced fantasy."
Buy at Bookshop.org
5

The Gods Will Sing Our Song

Autumn Krause
Historical fiction Ages 12 and up Starred · Publishers Weekly

Two Japanese American teenagers meet inside a World War II incarceration camp, where the hardship is entirely real and the Japanese mythology pressing in at the edges may be real too. Krause built the book out of her own family's history of internment.

For Lola: A romance set inside one of the ugliest things this country ever did to its own citizens, with old gods moving underneath it. Heavier than everything else on this week's list, and worth the weight.
September 1, 2026 · Delacorte · Starred review in Publishers Weekly
Buy at Bookshop.org

Thunder and Mercy

Jennifer Robin Barr
Historical fiction / Mystery Ages 10–14 Starred · School Library Journal

It's 1979, and twelve-year-old Theodora "Thunder" Underwood gets parked for the summer at Mount Pleasant, her aunt's crumbling mansion outside Philadelphia — a house once owned by Benedict Arnold. Then Thunder finds a diary kept by Mercy, a girl her own age who lived through the Revolution, and the two centuries start talking to each other. A shadowy prowler, a hidden ring and a tangle of spies pull both girls into the same secret.

For Lola: Two girls, two centuries, one mystery that only makes sense when you read them together. School Library Journal called it a story that "captures the freewheeling feel of a Philadelphia summer while weaving in high-stakes Revolutionary War espionage" — and if you liked Chasing Vermeer, Booklist thinks this is your next one.

Contemporary / Coming of age Ages 9–14 Starred · The Horn Book

Eleven-year-old Kestrel is uprooted from San Diego to Bozeman, Montana, where her mom takes a job at a bear institute caring for Karelian bear dogs. Afraid of the dogs, of the wide-open wilderness, and of her gruff grandfather, Kestrel has to find her footing far from everything she knows. Told in spare, fast-moving verse, it's a story about fear, freedom and figuring out where home really is.

For Lola: A big-hearted story you can read in one sitting, about a girl learning to trust herself in a place that scares her. If you love animals and a heroine who grows brave on her own terms, this one's for you.
